You are not there to sell everything. You are there to earn the next conversation.

The first conversation is not the Discovery Appointment and it is not the Decision Appointment. Build rapport, learn a little, give the merchant a reason to care and ask for time to learn more. Keep it short and sweet; respecting their time is part of the impression you make.

Build rapport

Take a genuine interest in the business and the person in front of you. Notice what is around you, like a signed jersey on the wall. People open up to people who are curious about them.

Say it

“Is that a signed jersey? There has to be a story behind that one.”

Ask a few questions

Get them talking about how they run things before you talk about Propelr. A few easy questions are enough; save the deep dive for the Discovery Appointment.

Say it

“Who handles your payments today? What do you like about them?”

Tell them why you're there

Give the merchant a reason to care. Local presence matters: if you just helped a business around the corner, say so. Keep the focus on them, not everything Propelr offers.

Say it

“I was just around the corner at [nearby business] and wanted to introduce myself.”

Ask for the appointment

Tell them why you want to come back, then offer 2 days at 2 different times. Never hand over an open calendar; people want to work with people who are busy and successful.

Say it

“I'd love to come back and see if there's a fit. Tuesday at 9am or Thursday at 2pm?”

At the Door

Learn enough to create relevance.

A few minutes, a few questions and one clear ask: the appointment.

At Discovery

Learn enough to recommend.

Understand the business, find the friction and earn the right to recommend.

Try it Out! (2 minutes or less) Volunteers, walk into a business and earn the appointment: build rapport, ask a few questions, tell them why you're there and ask for the meeting. Your merchant may respond with “I already have someone,” “I'm busy,” “Just send me something” or “We're happy with what we have.”
